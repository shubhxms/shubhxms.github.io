Stepwells are wells you walk down into. In a dry land they are a way of reaching water as the water table falls through the year, and the steps make the descent a kind of procession.{{< sidenote >}}TEST NOTE. Gujarat and Rajasthan have most of the surviving ones.{{< /sidenote >}}

## Water, then shade

The first job is water. The second, which is why people stayed, is shade: every storey down is a few degrees cooler than the one above it.

- A **kund** is a tank with steps on all sides.
- A **vav** is a long, stepped corridor down to a well shaft.
- A **baori** is the Rajasthani word for much the same.

## Carving

Every landing is a pavilion, and every pavilion is carved. Adalaj has elephants, dancers, a row of pots, and a frieze of the nine planets.{{< marginnote >}}TEST NOTE. An unnumbered margin note in a later section.{{< /marginnote >}}

## Decline

Piped water made them unnecessary, and the British thought them unhygienic. Many were filled in. The ones that survive are now monuments, which is better than being filled in and worse than being used.
